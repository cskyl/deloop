DeLoop: Repeated Actions as Self-Supervision for Long-Horizon Agents

Abstract

Language-model agents that run long tasks often redo work they have already done: they re-issue a tool call they made earlier and get back exactly the same observation, a step that adds no new information. We identify this failure as a loop. Loops appear natively in Qwen, Llama, and Gemini agents and become far more common once the agent's history is compressed to fit its context window.

To reduce these loops, we propose DeLoop, a self-supervised training method. DeLoop builds on a simple observation: the agent's own rollout can provide the supervision needed to learn from its loops. We use a looping action as a negative example and the first later non-looping action in the same rollout as a positive example. DeLoop learns from these pairs through direct preference optimization and does not require task rewards, gold answers, or LLM judges.

On CodeGym under compression, DeLoop reduces the share of Qwen3-8B tasks containing loops from 57.3% to 6.1%. It also uses about one third as many steps per task, with similar task success. On Qwen3-32B, DeLoop reduces loops and steps while raising task success from 37.7% to 44.8%.

What Is a Loop?

A loop occurs when the agent repeats a previous tool call with identical arguments and receives the same observation. Thus, that step adds no new information.

    Loops Are Costly, and Compression Makes Them More Common

    Loops are costly. On average over six Qwen3 and Gemini models, 59.5% of looping runs fail, compared with 33.4% of loop-free runs. Looping Qwen3-8B runs execute 10.5 more actions per task on average than loop-free runs and contain 11k more tokens.

    Compression makes loops more common. On CodeGym, Qwen3-8B loops in 29.0% of runs with the full history, 57.3% under summarization at 4,096 tokens, and 64.7% at 2,048 tokens. In 300 compressed histories, the agent repeats the most recent completed tool call in 3.3% of samples with its record present and 48.8% with it removed.

    Better context management does not close this gap. Summaries that keep an explicit record of past actions still loop in 52.0% to 62.7% of runs. The agent often fails to use a record it has, so we train the policy itself.

    Method: Repeated Actions as Self-Supervision

    To reduce looping under compressed context, we propose DeLoop, preference optimization on self-mined loop pairs.

    1. A. Detect loops. Step t is a repeat if its signature appeared earlier in the rollout.
    2. B. Mine pairs. The looping action is the rejected response , and the first later action that breaks the loop is the chosen response , both paired with the history before the loop, .
    3. C. Train with DPO. The policy learns to prefer the chosen action over the looping action under the same context, with the frozen base policy as the reference.

    Pair construction requires no task reward, gold label, or LLM judge, and a recovery provides a training pair even if the rollout ultimately fails the task.

    Model scaling

    Agents Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B, Qwen3-32B · Pairs mined from each model's own rollouts

    Loops fall at every scale, 3.5× on Qwen3-14B and 3.0× on Qwen3-32B. Tokens per task fall by 35% and 29%, respectively. At these scales DeLoop also raises task success: from 45.1% to 52.3% on Qwen3-14B and from 37.7% to 44.8% on Qwen3-32B.

    Cross-domain data, context settings, and memory policies

    DeLoop-Mix adds 4,635 cross-domain pairs from ALFWorld, Open-AgentRL, and Terminal-Bench, mined with the same label-free rule, and raises success both with and without compression (a). Across five context settings, the DeLoop loop rate remains between 4.7% and 12.8% while the untrained baseline reaches 65.3% (b). DeLoop-Mix lowers the loop rate under all seven memory policies; with an action ledger, from 62.7% to 11.3% (c).

    Other agent environments and benchmarks

    Loops fall in all four further environments, by up to 32.8 points on AppWorld, and tokens per task fall wherever recorded. In native harnesses, DeLoop cuts unproductive duplicate calls on Terminal-Bench from 16.6 to 6.9 per task, and DeLoop-Mix lowers the BrowseComp-Plus loop rate from 22.4% to 1.2% while matching the untrained model's accuracy. Trained only on CodeGym pairs, DeLoop on Qwen3-32B lowers the ALFWorld loop rate from 64.9% to 54.5% and raises task success from 33.6% to 42.5%.

    Conclusion

    Long-horizon agents that compress their history fall into loops: they re-issue an action they already ran and receive the same result. The loop labels itself: the looping action is the rejected action, and the agent's later loop-breaking action is the chosen one. An agent's own loops become its supervision: break the loop, keep the progress.
